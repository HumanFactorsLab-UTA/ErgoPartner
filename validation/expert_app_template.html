What we are asking you to do

You will see — images. Each one shows the same instant from three synchronised cameras — front, side and isometric — placed side by side. For each image you record what you observe about the worker's posture. Nothing is timed, and you can go back and change any answer before you export.

You record observations, not totals

You will not be asked to add anything up or to read a REBA or RULA table. You select the posture band you observe for each body segment, and this page composes the REBA and RULA scores from your selections using the same lookup tables as the system under test.

This is deliberate, for two reasons:

  • It removes clerical error. Hand-composing a score through three lookup tables is a well-known source of mistakes in reliability studies. A table slip would otherwise be recorded as a genuine disagreement with the system.
  • It isolates what we are actually measuring. Because both you and the system are composed by identical code, any remaining difference comes from the postural judgement itself — not from arithmetic, and not from a different version of a table.

It also gives us more than a total would. Because your segment-level judgements are recorded, we can see which body segment a disagreement lives in, rather than only that the final numbers differed. A total cannot be taken apart again afterwards.

The composed scores are shown to you as you work, so you can sanity-check a result that feels wrong. Enter what you observe first, then look.

Fixed inputs — please do not estimate these

Load, coupling and activity/muscle-use are given to you per image and are shown beside the questions. They are already fixed to the values the automated system used.

Please score them as stated, even where your own judgement of the scene would differ. Load and coupling cannot be read reliably from a still frame, and activity and muscle use depend on what happens before and after the instant you are shown. If you and the system assumed different loads, the comparison would measure that disagreement instead of the postural one we are trying to study.

Which side to score

Score the more severely loaded side — the side you would report if you were assessing this worker. If the two sides look equivalent, score either.

If an image cannot be scored

Some frames may be blurred, occluded, or caught mid-movement. Mark “I cannot score this frame” and give a brief reason rather than guessing. That is a result in itself and is more useful to us than a low-confidence score.
